# AFIT Market — Physical / Non-Coding Action Checklist

This is the real-world work the founder must do. Do not treat any item as completed just because the software supports it.

## A. AFIT permission and institutional sponsorship
Arrange a meeting with the appropriate AFIT leadership/administration and ask who has authority to approve a campus commerce and delivery pilot.

Ask:
1. Is a third-party campus marketplace permitted?
2. Which office owns approval: management, student affairs, commercial/works/estate, security, ICT, legal, or another unit?
3. Is a written pilot/MOU required? Who signs it?
4. Can vendors advertise and accept digital orders through an external platform?
5. Are there approved delivery hours, blackout periods, checkpoints or no-delivery zones?
6. Which campus destinations are approved for handover, and which are prohibited?
7. Can riders move between approved vendor/customer points? Are there escort, ID, vest, vehicle or access requirements?
8. Are motorcycles/bicycles allowed? Are there speed, parking, route or vehicle rules?
9. What identification can students, staff, military personnel and other authorized users use for eligibility checks?
10. What information must never be collected, mapped, photographed or displayed?
11. Can AFIT provide an approved list of public/operational delivery points? Who validates changes?
12. Who is the institutional escalation contact for security incidents?
13. What is the process for school events, exercises, inspections or sudden delivery suspensions?
14. What branding/name/logo permissions are needed to use “AFIT”?
15. Is AFIT willing to sponsor a small pilot and nominate a point of contact?

**Get in writing:** approval scope, approved pilot users/vendors, delivery hours, allowed zones/points, prohibited areas, incident escalation, branding permission and any data/security conditions.

## B. Security and military-environment questions
Do not request restricted operational details unless the authorized office decides they are necessary. Ask for safe operational rules instead:
- Which areas are no-delivery or require special approval?
- Which customer-facing delivery points are approved?
- Should the app display named zones or neutral labels?
- Are live maps permitted at all, and what level of map detail is allowed?
- Are rider live locations allowed to be visible to customers?
- Are photos/signatures allowed at delivery points?
- What identity checks are acceptable without storing sensitive military IDs?
- Are there time windows when location sharing or delivery must be disabled?
- What incident/reporting process should a rider follow if challenged or blocked?

## C. Vendor onboarding
Visit potential vendors and record:
- Owner/authorized contact
- Shop name and permitted location
- Product categories
- Menu/catalogue and prices
- Opening hours and closure dates
- Preparation/fulfillment estimates
- Stock/availability process
- Bank account and settlement details (collect securely, never in casual chat)
- Vendor commission/fees and payout timetable
- Cancellation/refund/return policy
- Food hygiene, packaging and allergen responsibilities where relevant
- Agreement to platform terms, customer privacy and dispute cooperation

Start with a manageable pilot set of vendors rather than trying to onboard everyone.

## D. Rider operations
Define:
- Who can become a rider and who approves them
- Age/eligibility and identity checks that are lawful and proportionate
- Vehicle type, licensing/insurance requirements if applicable
- Training on app, customer conduct, delivery zones, privacy and safety
- Rider compensation and delivery-fee allocation
- What happens if a rider goes offline mid-route
- What happens if a rider has five jobs and one becomes delayed
- Package handover procedure at vendor
- Recipient unavailable procedure and safe waiting limits
- Incident, damage, theft, accident and harassment reporting
- Return-to-vendor process and who pays return delivery
- Suspension/appeal policy
- Whether rider uniforms/ID badges are required by AFIT

Get signed rider terms and conduct/privacy acknowledgement before activation.

## E. Payment provider and finance
Before accepting real payments:
1. Open/verify the correct business account with Paystack or chosen provider.
2. Explain that this is a multi-vendor marketplace and ask which supported product/contract permits the intended flow.
3. Ask whether delayed settlement pending delivery confirmation is supported; do not assume transaction splitting means escrow.
4. Ask how subaccounts, vendor verification, refunds, partial refunds, chargebacks and disputes work.
5. Ask who bears processing fees and how split payments interact with refunds.
6. Confirm settlement timelines, limits and required business documents.
7. Have a Nigerian legal/compliance professional review the funds flow, marketplace terms and any holding/releasing mechanism.
8. Test only in sandbox until approved.
9. Define bookkeeping/reconciliation and refund approval roles.
10. Never promise vendors a payout guarantee the provider cannot support.

## F. Legal, privacy and insurance
Consult an appropriately qualified Nigerian lawyer/privacy adviser about:
- Business registration and platform terms
- AFIT institutional permission and use of name/branding
- Vendor and rider contracts
- Consumer protection and pricing/refund disclosures
- Electronic signature evidence and receipt wording
- Nigeria Data Protection Act obligations and whether any registration/assessment is required
- Data retention for signatures, location, identity and disputes
- Liability for lost/damaged goods, food quality, accidents and rider conduct
- Insurance requirements
- Payment provider terms, chargebacks and settlement
- Returns for food, hygiene-sensitive and perishable goods

Prepare:
- Privacy notice
- Terms of use
- Vendor agreement
- Rider agreement
- Refund/cancellation/return policy
- Delivery and safety policy
- Dispute escalation policy
- Data retention/deletion schedule
- Incident response contact list

## G. Map and delivery-point preparation
Only after AFIT authorizes it:
- Walk the permitted routes with an authorized representative.
- Create a list of approved customer-facing delivery points.
- Validate names and map pins.
- Record accessibility, opening-hour and rider access constraints.
- Mark prohibited zones internally without exposing sensitive details.
- Test GPS accuracy on different Android devices and network conditions.
- Decide what the customer map is allowed to show.
- Agree who can update delivery points and how updates are approved.

Do not photograph or map restricted facilities or operational areas without explicit authorization.

## H. Pilot preparation
Before launch:
- Recruit a small number of vendors, riders and test users.
- Run fake-money and test-payment drills.
- Simulate missing item, wrong item, recipient unavailable, rider offline, GPS failure, QR replay, signature refusal, return and partial refund.
- Train vendor staff on the simple pickup/handover screen.
- Train riders on Go Active, location privacy, batching up to five orders and incident reporting.
- Provide customer help/contact path.
- Decide pilot hours, geographic scope, order cap and daily stop conditions.
- Have an emergency pause procedure for all deliveries.
- Obtain explicit approval before any public launch or live payment activity.
